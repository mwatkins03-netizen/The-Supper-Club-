The Supper Club

Two AI models, Sage and Jev, read the same historic menu scans and tell you how sure they are. You get to read the menus too, and argue with both of them.

First courseHead to head

Pick a menu, pick up to eight questions, and send the scan to both models at once. Each answer comes back as a probability, not a sentence. Where the models disagree, read the menu yourself and decide who is closer.

After dinnerAbout the table

    
A prototype for teaching calibrated AI: models that answer with a number you can check, and that sometimes decline to answer.

  

  

    

      
The two models

      
Sage (Levanto, levanto-sage-2) answers yes/no, choice and scale questions with calibrated probabilities. When the top answers are too close, it returns no answer; this site shows that as "Sage declines."

      
Jev (TypeSafe, Jev 1.14) answers the same kinds of questions with probabilities and always commits to an answer, however split the odds.

      
Neither model writes prose here. They read the scan and return numbers. That makes their doubt visible, which is the point.

    

    

      
The menus

      
Scans are from the Buttolph Collection at the New York Public Library, gathered by Frank E. Buttolph and digitized through NYPL's crowdsourced What's on the Menu? project.

      
The catalogue of 6,273 menus (title, place, year, page images) comes from The Pudding's 5,000 Restaurant Menus, 1880–1920 (MIT license).

    

    

      
Privacy and cost

      
Menu scans are sent to Levanto and TypeSafe through small proxies that hold the API keys. Your answers, reflections and saved questions stay in this browser (localStorage) until you export them.

      
Each scan costs about 1,400 input tokens per model per call.

      
Palette

      
Sanzo Wada, A Dictionary of Color Combinations (1933), No. 290: Vistoris Lake, Pale Lemon Yellow, Lincoln Green, Cobalt Green, chosen from the Combination Index.
